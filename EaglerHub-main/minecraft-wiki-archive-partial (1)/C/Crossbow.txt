A crossbow is a ranged weapon that can load and fire arrows, arrow variants, and firework rockets. Holding use loads it and it remains loaded until it is fired, after which it can be loaded again.

Mob loot
Pillagers
When killed, a pillager has a small chance (8.5%) of dropping a loaded or unloaded crossbow with random durability; the drop rate increases by 1% per level of Looting: 9.5% with Looting I, 10.5% with Looting II and 11.5% with Looting III. The dropped crossbow can rarely (10% chance) be enchanted at level 5–19 regardless of being enchanted when it spawned.

Piglins
Piglins have a chance of wielding a crossbow upon spawning, and they also have a small chance (8.5%) of dropping a loaded or unloaded crossbow of random durability when killed; this rate increases by 1% per level of Looting: 9.5% with Looting I, 10.5% with Looting II and 11.5% with Looting III. It can rarely (10% chance) be enchanted at level 5–19 regardless of being enchanted when spawned.

Usage
Attacking
Shooting a crossbow in Java Edition.
Crossbows are similar to bows, but can be charged in advance for use at any time. They take longer to charge than a bow, are slightly more powerful, and shoot farther with slightly higher accuracy.
Crossbows can be loaded with arrows of any type. Like the bow, arrows shot by crossbows can be blocked by shields. A crossbow enchanted with Piercing fires arrows that ignore blocking shields, allowing them to pass through and still hit their target. Arrows shot from a crossbow enchanted with Piercing can be picked up and used again, creating an effect similar to the bow-exclusive Infinity enchantment. When an arrow hits another player, the player who fired the arrow hears a high-pitched "ding" sound to indicate the successful shot.‌[Java Edition  only]
Alternatively, they can be loaded with a firework rocket, which flies in a straight line at 32 m/s and, in Java Edition, explodes on contact with blocks and entities. If the firework rocket has no firework stars, it deals no damage. In order to use a firework rocket with a crossbow, at least one firework rocket must be present in the player's offhand. Using a firework rocket uses 3 durability per shot, and fireworks with higher flight duration travel farther when fired. When a crossbow has the Multishot enchantment, the increased durability usage stacks, resulting in 9 durability being consumed with each shot. Piercing does not work with firework rockets, and firework rocket explosions have a radius of roughly 4 blocks.
A crossbow can first be loaded by using it, which starts charging it. While charging the crossbow, the player moves at sneaking speed. When the crossbow finishes charging, the arrow or firework rocket is then loaded into it and the player’s speed returns to normal. At this point, the crossbow must be used a second time to fire its projectile. Shooting a loaded crossbow does not delay any of the player's current actions like sprinting or swimming. Both arrows and firework rockets launched by crossbows are not augmented by the Strength effect.
In Java Edition, continuing to hold use after firing a crossbow causes it to begin recharging again. In Bedrock Edition, continuing to hold use after firing a crossbow does not cause it to begin recharging, as the input must be released and then inputed again. There is also a small delay after fully charging a crossbow where it cannot be fired despite being fully charged, and a similar delay after firing a crossbow where it cannot be recharged again. These bugs cause crossbows to effectively charge and fire slower than in Java Edition.
A loaded crossbow keeps its projectile when placed in another inventory slot or into an item frame without the projectile being unloaded or fired. A loaded crossbow also remains charged after repairing or enchanting it using a grindstone, anvil, or enchanting table. In Java Edition, the crossbow’s tooltip also lists the loaded projectile and its effect, if any. Like with bows, the player can choose the type of arrow to fire based on its location in their inventory:

Arrows in the off-hand, or the selected hotbar slot if the crossbow is in the off-hand, are prioritized first.
If there are no arrows in the off-hand or selected hotbar slot, the arrow closest to slot 0 is selected.
Some mobs cannot be shot using an arrow, because the arrow either disappears or is deflected. However, by use of the explosion from an indirect firework rocket, these arrow-immune mobs can be damaged:

Endermen - even while immobile, the enderman is immune to damage from arrows;
Breeze;
Wither under half health, when it gains wither armor;
Closed shulkers;
Ender dragon, while sitting on top of the exit portal.

A crossbow cannot be enchanted with Power, Punch, Flame, or Infinity in Survival mode. However, they can be applied via anvil in Creative mode in Java Edition or by using commands. When applied, each enchantment functions the same for the crossbow as it would for a bow.

 Due to a server-client desync bug, crossbows can occasionally be fired 1 tick earlier than their programmed 1.25 second charge time.

 Multishot and Piercing are mutually exclusive.

Fuel
Crossbows can be used as fuel in furnaces, smelting 1.5 items per crossbow.
Despite using iron ingots in their crafting recipe, crossbows cannot be smelted into iron nuggets.

Trivia
Alongside pillagers, crossbows were inspired by Minecraft Dungeons, even though they were added in the base game first.
Crossbows enchanted with Quick Charge VI and above get stuck on the first frame on the recharging animation because the resulting charge time is negative, and the game never reaches the time markers that tell it to advance the animation or the reloading procedure itself. It is technically possible to charge such crossbows, but this would require overflowing the value that determines how long the crossbow has been charged. This takes such an amount of time that this is practically impossible: for example, charging a crossbow with Quick Charge VI would take 69.4 years to complete.
Although Piercing allows arrows to bypass shields, they cannot get through the wither's projectile armor or a shulker's shell.
In real life, crossbows do not shoot the same arrows as bows, rather they shoot shorter, thicker, and oftentimes fletchless arrows (usually called "bolts") made to withstand the crossbow's increased power, which can shatter conventional arrows.
