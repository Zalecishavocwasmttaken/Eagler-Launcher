This article is about music played in Minecraft.   For the command, see Commands/music.   For the unique music in Legacy Console Edition, see Music (Legacy Console Edition).  For other uses, see Music (disambiguation).

A jukebox playing otherside
Minecraft contains many music tracks, which play in various places in the game, including on the main menu, in Creative mode, in the Nether, in the End, and during the credits. Certain tracks from music discs can also be played with a jukebox. The music tracks in the game are composed by C418, Lena Raine, Kumi Tanioka, Samuel Åberg, Aaron Cherof, Amos Roddy, Hyper Potions, and fingerspit.

Playing
Background music
Music tracks can play at various points during gameplay. Some types of music are triggered by events, while others play randomly while certain criteria are met.

If the player is in the menu screens (not in a world), a random menu track starts playing after 1 to 30 seconds. Menu music stops playing in Java Edition when the player enters the loading world screen, or fades out in Bedrock Edition when the loading screen finishes.
If the player is in the Overworld and not in Creative, a random general track plays which depends on the biome.
If the player is in the Overworld and is currently in Creative mode, a random track plays from the Creative music (as well as regular in Java Edition).
If the player is underwater in an ocean or river biome, random underwater tracks play.
If the player is in the Nether, a random general Nether track or a Nether biome-specific track plays, except warped forests, which don't have any music.
If the player is in the End and the ender dragon has been defeated, the normal End track plays.
The song "Boss" always plays instantly while the player is in the End if the ender dragon is undefeated or resummoned.
The song "Alpha" always plays instantly during the credits.
If a music track is playing while the player is entering a pale garden, it fades into silence. The music resumes when the player exits the biome.

Game music stops playing when the player quits the world, or fades out in Bedrock Edition. When the game is paused, game music continues to play in Java Edition, while in Bedrock Edition it pauses and resumes when the player exits the game menu or disables pausing.A music toast in Java Edition
The regular, Creative, Nether, End, and underwater gameplay music randomly plays 10 to 20 minutes[verify] after currently playing tracks are finished. In Java Edition, the player can change how frequently music plays by changing the "Music Frequency" setting in the "Music & Sounds" menu.
In Java Edition, when the "Show Music Toast" setting is enabled in the "Music & Sounds" menu, a toast showing the track name and artist appears on-screen when a new track starts playing. This toast is also shown in the game menu.

Music discs
Main article: Music Disc
An additional 22 tracks in Minecraft are exclusive to music discs. These are rare items obtained through specific loot chests or mobs killed in a specific way. A music disc can be played in a jukebox block and can be heard up to 64 blocks away by any player.
For a full list of jukebox tracks, see Music Disc.

Technical details
The player can force specific music tracks to play using the /playsound command with the track's ID. In Bedrock Edition, the /music command can also be used and allows to set a queue, or stop only music sounds.
All music as well as sounds in Minecraft are stored in the sound directory. In Java Edition, however, the sound files are obfuscated and cannot be directly accessed. To get around with this, the sound directory can be extracted by the methods showed in Tutorial:Sound directory. In Bedrock Edition, the music files are not obfuscated and can be found directly at Content\data\resource_packs\vanilla_music\sounds\music.
The song name shown in the music toast is found by converting the sound file's resource ID into a translation key that can be looked up in the current language file. Note that this is the sound file, not the sound event. To form a translation key, all path and namespace separators in the resource ID are replaced with periods. For example, a resource ID like custom_namespace:music/game/custom_song would use the translation key custom_namespace.music.game.custom_song. If the song is in the vanilla minecraft namespace, the namespace is not included, so the vanilla song minecraft:music/game/creative/blind_spots uses the translation key music.game.creative.blind_spots.

Composers
Originally, all of Minecraft's music was composed by C418. According to Mojang, as the game got more popular (specifically starting with the Update Aquatic), others were hired to produce music and soundtracks for the game. However, C418 has cited legal disputes between himself and Microsoft, who wanted to own the music he produced outright, as the real reason for additional composers. Since the Nether Update, new tracks composed by Lena Raine, Kumi Tanioka, Samuel Åberg, Aaron Cherof, Amos Roddy, Hyper Potions and fingerspit were added. Samuel Åberg also composed many recent ambient sounds. Famous music producer and DJ Deadmau5 was also meant to make music for the game alongside C418, but his works didn't make it into Minecraft for unknown reasons.

Trivia



It has been suggested that  this section be split into its own page. [discuss]
If this split affects many pages, or may potentially be controversial, do not split until a consensus has been reached. Reason: a lot of these should be on the individual songs' pages and not here


"The End" is heavily built on modified versions of several tracks from "Minecraft - Volume Alpha".
"calm3.ogg", or "Sweden", is a Passacaglia, with a changing melody over a repeated bass line.
On April Fools' Day 2014, Mojang played a bunch of villager themed pranks on the players, including temporarily replacing the track "hal1.ogg", or "Subwoofer Lullaby", with a version that had a villager singing along, as well as temporarily replacing all the menu songs with the villagers singing the Game Of Thrones theme song. This was reverted on April 2, 2014.
hal3.ogg (Haggstrom) is in septuple meter (precisely 7/4 time).
According to C418, the track "Mice on Venus" (game filename is piano3.ogg) was originally a short piano solo, but was then changed later in Minecraft's development and an addendum was added, which made the track what the game has currently.
Some of the music tracks have a special title:
The word "Shuniji" (主虹, しゅにじ) means "primary rainbow" in Japanese.
The title of the track "So Below" is a reference to "As above, so below", a phrase associated with Hermeticism that is derived from a passage from the Emerald Tablet of Thoth, relating to the secrets of creating the philosopher's stone.
The word rubedo is Latin for "redness". The term was adopted by alchemists to refer to the final stage of creating the philosopher's stone.
The word chrysopoeia (χρυσοποιία) is an alchemical term that describes the creation of the philosopher's stone, or the transmutation of a given object into gold.
"Bromeliad" refers to a taxonomic family of flowering plants. This family contains pitcher plants and species resembling torchflowers.

Notes

 In Java Edition, rather than checking the player's gamemode directly, the game checks for both the instabuild and mayfly player abilities and plays Creative music if both are true. Both are normally enabled upon entering Creative mode, but can be enabled or disabled individually by third-party means.

 "A Familiar Room" does not play in Survival mode on Bedrock Edition due to a bug.
