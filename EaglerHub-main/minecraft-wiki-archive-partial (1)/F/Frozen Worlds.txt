Frozen Worlds, also known as Frozen Planet II: Frozen Worlds, is a lesson in Minecraft Education and a free Marketplace map in Bedrock Edition. As part of the Frozen Planet II lesson series, the map was inspired by BBC Earth's documentary series Frozen Planet II, and introduces animal species within various polar regions.

Summary
The player spawns in a small room with blue walls, a jungle planks floor, and white corners and ceiling. The two corners with a door in between hold two flower pots, and the door leads to a black void teleporting the player to the Arctic.

Polar bear
There, the sea is covered in icy floes made up of snow while the land consists of snowy mountains. The narrator describes the Arctic itself before narrating the life of a polar bear mother teaching her cubs to adapt to sea ice. The player in this minigame will play the role of this bear, who must guide her two cubs across the floes while following beacon beam checkpoints. These floes are barely connected with thin strips of ice. However, male polar bears thhreaten the cubs, and the player can attack them to ward them off temporarily. If any of the cubs get attacked, the player is teleported to the last checkpoint they reached prior to attack. After completing the minigame, the player is teleported to research base in human form.

Research base
There, the player spawns in one of the tents and can use the camera to take pictures of a polar bear, its cubs, and a walrus, before teleporting to Antarctica.

Killer whale
Antartica in this minigame is made up of snow and frozen rivers, which the narrator describes as "devoid of life". In the more populated Southern Ocean, a pod of killer whales are led by a matriarch across the ocean during hunts. As the matriarch killer whale, the player must gather the other members of its pod by passing through rings, but only the player's killer whale can gather others. After gathering five members, the player has to knock the ice floe over their head by tapping the green area of the bar when the arrow reaches it. This causes the entire pod to knock a Weddell seal off the floe and into the ocean. Repeating this two more times will win the minigame, which teleports the player spawns outside the research base.

Research base
There, they have to travel around the area to take pictures of a killer whale, a chinstrap penguin, and a Weddell seal, after which they get teleported to the northern tundra.

Lapland bumblebee
The tundra is at the stage of thawing at the beginning of spring, causing plant life to be revealed. Deep in a hole beneath the surface is a Lapland bumblebee queen who awakened from her slumber after the rest of her colony died from the winter cold. According to the narrator, her thick fur and anti-freeze resistance helped her survive the cold, and eventually thaws to black after being icy blue. The minigame consists of aiding her warm the eggs in their nest and gather nectar outside the burrow 20 times for three minutes. The temperature bar indicates that of the eggs, lowering as they cool down over time but rising after interacting with the nest. While gathering nectar, a hunger bar appears next to the bee, which decreases over time and can be refilled by collecting nectar. Flowers with nectar available have particles floating above them, which disappear after collecting it. Gathering all nectar wins the game, after which the baby bees hatch and emerge from the burrow and the player is teleported to base.

Research base
Across the forest, they must take pictures of a Siberian tiger, an erosion occuring at the riverbank, and the footprints of an Amur leopard. The third also requires placing a field camera and leave it to take a picture of the leopard, which takes a few seconds before the player can collect the picture and add it to the journal.

Ending
After gathering all four pictures, they have to talk to the researcher at the base, who explains the danger of climate change on polar species. Afterward, the screen turns black, showing the key artwork and a congratulations message to share. Skipping this will reveal the credits in a tundra background.

Researcher's statement

Hello, I see you gathered all of the research material. Can you give it to me?
Did you know that the world is now about 1.2C warmer that it was in the 19th century? The amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere has risen by 50%.
The more our climate changes, the more impact it could have on animals like the bumblebee and leopard. The suitable habitat for leopards is shrinking because of destruction to their habitat largely caused by humans. Climate change also threatens to reduce suitable forest habitat and availability of prey.
Human interference and temperature rises must slow down if we want to avoid the worst consequences for these animals.
Sometimes I feel sad when I think about the environmental problems we're facing. It helps me to remember that people all over the world are working together to make a difference. Protection has already helped Amur leopards to avoid extinction and their numbers are currently growing, which gives me hope!
Humans can help to  [sic] steady the climate by reducing emissions. Reusing products, reducing waste and recycling waste are all practices that can lead to emissions reductions.
Let's work together to help save these amazing animals!

Trivia
The minigames of Frozen Worlds were reused in its sequels: the polar bear game in Frozen Ocean, the killer whale game in Frozen South, and the Lapland bumblebee game in Frozen Lands.
Additionally, the research games were reused on their respective maps and the researcher's statement is also present in Frozen Lands.
