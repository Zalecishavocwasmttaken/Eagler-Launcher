In-game colored block/item spectrum
Dyes are a set of sixteen items used to change the color of wool, wool stairs​[upcoming: Third Drop 2026], wool slabs​[upcoming: Third Drop 2026], cushions​[upcoming: Third Drop 2026], carpets, terracotta, concrete powder, glass, shulker boxes, beds, candles, bundles, harnesses, the patterns on banners, firework stars, certain mobs, and text on signs and hanging signs. In Bedrock Edition, they can be used to dye water in a cauldron (which is thereafter used to dye leather armor) and shulkers; in Java Edition, leather armor can be dyed directly. In Minecraft Education, they can be used to dye balloons and glow sticks.
In Bedrock Edition and Minecraft Education, bone meal, ink sacs, lapis lazuli, and cocoa beans can generally substitute for white dye, black dye, blue dye, and brown dye, respectively, in crafting recipes and for use in dyeing items or mobs. However, they have other important uses that are not related to color, and are therefore not considered true dyes. They are mentioned in this article only regarding their use as dyeing agents; see their individual articles for complete information about them.

Obtaining
A chart showing which pairs of dyes can be used to craft other dyes
Most dyes can be crafted from flowers and a few other items; some dyes can be crafted by combining multiple dyes of different colors, and every dye is sold by the wandering trader. Some dyes can be found in loot, and green dye and lime dye are obtained by smelting. Purple dye is the only dye where the only method of being obtained is by crafting from other dyes. In Bedrock Edition, tertiary colors, along with their regular crafting recipes, can be crafted with primary colors. For example, magenta can be crafted with one red dye and two white dyes.

Mob loot
In Bedrock Edition, wandering traders have a chance of 8.5% (increased by 1% per level of Looting) to drop 3 dyes when killed while showing the item as trade offer, if the player holds an emerald.

Leather armor, wolf armor, leather horse armor can be dyed:

In Bedrock and Legacy Console editions, armor is dyed by using it on a cauldron filled with dyed water. This consumes 1 layer of water but does not change the color of the water.
The armor can be undyed by using it on a cauldron with undyed water. This does not change the water's color to the color of the armor, and changes the armor to have no dye color.
Adding a dye to dyed water mixes the color of the water with the color of the dye, as 2 colors (using the formula below). The game does not remember which colors were placed into the cauldron. It just mixes the current color with the added dye, using the formula below. So, blue + pink + blue + pink is not the same as pink + blue + pink + blue.
In Java Edition, it is possible to craft more than one dye with the armor, and armor can be dyed multiple times with previous colors affecting the outcome.
Colored armor can be reverted to its original color by using it on a cauldron with undyed water.
Armor retains its enchantments and name when dyed or undyed.

Dye mixing formula
Each edition of the game uses a specific formula for calculating the color of dyed armor. In this formula, the color of armor or cauldron water, and each dye color, in the RGB color model, has a red value, green value, and blue value.

Java Edition
In Java Edition, dyes can be applied to a piece of leather armor using crafting.
Suppose each color is a point on the coordinate plane, c=(r,g,b). This means that:

r (the red  component) is c.x
g (the green component) is c.y
b (the blue  component) is c.z
The formula to mix a list of colors, cs, is this:

f(cs)=mean⁡(cs)⋅mean⁡(max⁡(cs.x,cs.y,cs.z))max⁡(mean⁡(cs.x),mean⁡(cs.y),mean⁡(cs.z))
Notes:

If the armor is dyed, its color is the first element in cs.
If the armor is undyed, it has no color.
The color values of all of the dyes used in the crafting recipe make up the rest of the element in cs.
Since the crafting table grid only has 9 slots 1 to 8 dyes can be used, so cs can have anywhere from 1 to 9 element. If the player uses the 2×2 grid, then only 1 to 3 dyes can be used, and cs will have 1 to 4 element.
That formula could also be split up like this:

pmax(c)=max⁡(c.x,c.y,c.z)
mean⁡(cs)⋅mean⁡(pmax(cs))pmax(mean⁡(cs))
In the split up formula, mean⁡(pmax(cs)) is called the average maximum, and pmax(mean⁡(cs)) is called the maximum average. The average maximum divided by the maximum average is the alpha factor.

α=mean⁡(pmax(cs))pmax(mean⁡(cs))
Or without the pmax function: mean⁡(max⁡(cs.x,cs.y,cs.z))max⁡(total⁡(cs.x)cs.length,total⁡(cs.y)cs.length,total⁡(cs.z)cs.length)
For this formula, the exact order of floating point operations matters.

x⋅yz is not the same as x⋅yz.
fa(cs) cannot be written as mean⁡(cs)⋅mean⁡(pmax(cs))pmax(mean⁡(cs)), because some mix operations would give slightly different colors.

Bedrock Edition
In Bedrock Edition, dyes cannot be applied to armor directly and are applied to cauldron water instead. When a dye is used on undyed cauldron water, the water becomes dyed, and will have the color of that dye. When a dye is used on dyed cauldron water, a simple formula is
cnext=⌊ccurrent+cdye2⌋
Notes: (when adding a single dye to dyed cauldron water)

cnext is the color that the dyed water will have after the dye is added.
ccurrent is the color of the dyed water before the dye is added.
cdye is the color of the dye.
Bedrock Edition encodes each of these colors as an ARGB integer, which can also be thought of as a list of four integers.

The first  byte of the integer is the alpha component (A) of the color.
The second byte of the integer is the red   component (R) of the color.
The third  byte of the integer is the green component (G) of the color.
The fourth byte of the integer is the blue  component (B) of the color.
A value of x in any given component corresponds to a value of y=rounddown⁡(255⋅x) in the RGBA format.
The color values of the dye items are stored in the RGBA format. To convert a value of x in the RGBA format to a float for the mixing algorithm, the formula x=y255 is used.

Dyeing sheep
Dyes can be used on sheep to change the color of the wool. Shearing a colored sheep drops the corresponding color of the wool, and the sheep retains the color when the wool regenerates. Killing colored sheep drops one block of wool with the corresponding color. Breeding colored sheep produce a lamb colored as one of the parent sheep, or a color resulting from the combination of both parents' colors. The color combining follows the same rules that dyes use – red and yellow sheep produce an orange lamb, but a blue and yellow sheep cannot create a green lamb. In case the colors of the colored sheep do not mix, the color of a lamb is randomly chosen between the two parents with equal chance. The unlimited reproduction of colored sheep makes dyeing and shearing sheep infinitely more efficient than just dyeing wool directly.

Dyeing collars
Dye can be used on a tamed wolf or cat to change the color of its collar from the default red to the color of the dye.

Dyeing signs
Dye can be used on a sign or a hanging sign to change the text color. In Bedrock Edition, ink sacs cannot be used for this purpose; black dye must be used to change the text to black. A sign cannot be dyed if its color is already the color of the dye. Attempting to dye it will not use the dye and will open the menu to edit the sign, if possible. A sign defaults to black text when placed.

Color values
The "color codes" are used to determine the color imparted on banners, dyed leather armor, leather horse armor and wolf armor, wolf and cat collars, beacon beams, tropical fish, sheep, and firework stars. The hex value is shown in the extended tooltips of dyed armor; however, to set the color using an NBT data tag in a command, the decimal value must be used instead. The color values for firework stars are slightly different. In Java Edition, sheep's wool uses different colors than the base dye values, appearing noticeably darker.
Color codes are usually encoded using the corresponding numeric ID, although e.g. signs store the color name instead to represent text color.

Trivia
Players can obtain each one of every color with 1 cocoa bean, 2 dandelions/wildflowers or 1 sunflower, 2 ink sacs or wither roses, 3 cactuses, 4 lapis lazuli or 4 cornflowers, 4 small red flowers (for example, 4 poppies) or 2 rose bushes, and 6 bone meal.
