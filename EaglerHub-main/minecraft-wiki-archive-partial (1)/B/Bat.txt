A bat is a flying passive mob that spawns in dark areas. It is the only ambient mob in the game, intended to complement the game's atmospheric settings.

Spawning
Bats can spawn in groups of 8‌[JE  only]​[upcoming: BE 26.50] or 2‌[BE  only]​[until: BE 26.50] in the Overworld at a light level of 3 or less in Java Edition and a light level of 4 or less in Bedrock Edition​[upcoming: BE 26.50] at any Y-level, on blocks of stone, granite, diorite, andesite, tuff, or deepslate that are not directly exposed to the sky. They also need at least 1.5 air blocks (with for instance, a top trapdoor or top slab in the block above foot level). In Java Edition, they can spawn in any Overworld biome except the deep dark.​[upcoming: BE 26.50] In Bedrock Edition, there are also other specific biomes they cannot spawn in.​[until: BE 26.40]
Bat entities do not count toward the passive mob cap, as they are considered ambient creatures in the entity inheritance, and therefore fall into the dedicated ambient category.

Drops
On death
Bats do not drop any items or experience upon death.

Behavior
Bats often fly around aimlessly, squeaking randomly. Their flight pattern tends to favor the east, and sometimes they may fly into lava and catch on fire.
Bats hang upside down on the underside of a solid block when idle, though they cannot hang on transparent or non-solid blocks. During this state, they squeak far less and occasionally turn to face different directions. They remain in this state until they are disturbed; if a player approaches a bat hanging upside down, even if the player is invisible, it flies away. This also happens if the player breaks the block the bat is hanging from.
Bats despawn when over 32 blocks away from the player.
Bats' collision boxes do not interact with the player or redstone components‌[Java Edition  only].
Bats cannot be transported by conventional means. A lead cannot be attached to a bat, and it cannot be put in a minecart‌[Java Edition  only] or boat.
Bats can also get caught in and slowed by cobwebs.
Bats cannot breed, and baby bats do not exist.

Trivia
According to Jens Bergensten, the bat is an example of an atmospheric/ambient mob, that is, a mob that constantly spawns, similar to the squid, but also constantly despawns because when a player is not around it does not serve any purpose.
The updated texture of the bat was created in roughly 2 hours. However, the model and animations took longer.
During Minecraft: The Island, there is a scene where Guy ends up wasting his final arrow when it accidentally hit a bat instead of a creeper during a pivotal moment. According to author Max Brooks, this was something that actually happened to him while playing.
In Java Edition, bats are the only mob in the "Ambient" mob cap category.
