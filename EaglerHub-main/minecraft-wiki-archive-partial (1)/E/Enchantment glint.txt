The enchantment glint is a visual effect applied most often to enchanted items and equipment, as well as some other items.

Summary
The enchantment glint is shown on any item that has been enchanted as well as some items naturally, namely enchanted golden apples, nether stars, end crystals, bottles o' enchanting, lodestone compasses, written books, enchanted books, and debug sticks.
Enchanted armor, elytra, and carved pumpkins also display the enchantment glint when equipped.​[more information needed] As do horse armor, nautilus armor, wolf armor, carpets, saddles, and harnesses when equipped as armor by mobs.
Eyes of ender enchanted through commands will appear enchanted when thrown.​[more information needed]
Chests equipped by donkeys, mules, and llamas do not display the effect. Held or framed maps also do not display it.

Animation generation

Unlike most animations in the game, which are defined through mcmeta files, the enchantment glint's generation is hardcoded and cannot be fully modified. The color and texture of the enchantment glint can be modified with resource packs in Java Edition versions greater than 1.15. Resource packs in Bedrock Edition can also modify the enchantment glint but green, yellow, and white colors do not render well due to a different rendering engine.
Interestingly, the enchantment glint's rendering is tied to the size of the atlas. This causes unique visual effects, for example, if all of the game's textures are unloaded:

Obtaining
   For the list of enchantment glint owning items, see Enchantment glint § Summary.   For enchantments in general, see Enchantment.

There are 3 ways to get an item with an enchantment glint:

Items that have enchantment glint naturally
Enchanting items
Giving them the NBT component: minecraft:enchantment_glint_override‌[JE  only].
To obtain a stick with an enchantment glint, use the following command: /give @p stick[minecraft:enchantment_glint_override=true]‌[JE  only].
